# lofu app (macOS, iOS, web)

The lofu app on [Native Shell](https://nsh.dev) (nsh 0.4.0): one TSX codebase that runs as an AppKit app on macOS, a UIKit app on iOS, and in the browser. It started as a prototype answering **can an nsh app show a saved article, let you highlight passages, and put the highlights back in the right place?** (yes, see Findings).

- **Layout:** on a Mac (or any window 640+ points wide, not a phone) a floating sidebar of Liquid Glass sits beside a card grid whose toolbar floats over it, in a window with the app's own title bar. On a phone it's a native navigation stack that opens on *Library* (`screens/Home.tsx`), which has round glass buttons for settings and a menu (new collection, fold or unfold every section, sort collections and tags, all kept on the device), inset groups for collections, filters (favorites, highlights and the kinds of page: articles, videos, documents…) and tags that fold away, the Trash last, and search and *+* on glass at the bottom. Typing in its search narrows the page to the collections, filters and tags whose names match, under a row that searches the bookmarks' text.
- **Library:** *All bookmarks*, *Unsorted*, *Favorites*, *Highlights*, collections and tags, with counts, and the *Trash* at the foot of the sidebar or the Library page. Cards show the page's cover, or a catalog card with the excerpt in serif when it has none. Pages the server couldn't read are marked *Unreadable*. Right-click (desktop) or long-press (phone) for Open original, Copy link, Share…, Favorite, Edit, Move to (a collection or Unsorted), Try reading it again, and Delete. On iOS that's the system's own menu in Liquid Glass (`UIMenu`, `PopupMenu` in `src/lib.rs`), growing out of where the finger is, a swipe's *Move* and *More*, the reader's •••, or the Library page's ••• (with the sorts as submenus, the current one checked): grouped, with symbols, and the collections as a submenu of *Move to*. It is the menu of a clear `UIButton` laid there while it shows (a button's menu is glass and leaves the screen undimmed). UIKit has no public call that opens a button's menu, so the app asks the button's `UIContextMenuInteraction` with `_presentMenuAtLocation:`; should a future iOS drop that call, the app falls back to nsh's action sheet.
- **Swipes (phones):** a row swiped right shows *Move* (*Put back* in the Trash); swiped left, *Share…*, *Delete* and *More* (`components/SwipeRow.tsx`). The drag is a native pan bound to the horizontal axis only, so the list still scrolls; one row is open at a time and a tap shuts it.
- **Trash:** *Delete* moves a bookmark to the Trash, which is what the server calls `archived` (bookmarks archived before are there too). Deleting from the Trash asks, then deletes it for good: `DELETE /v1/bookmarks/{id}`, a tombstone that other devices drop on sync. Search leaves the Trash out, except in the Trash.
- **System controls:** on iOS 26 and macOS 26 the round glass buttons (*+*, Back, Close), the labelled ones (*Open original*, *Cancel*) and, on iOS, the search field are the system's own, registered from Rust as native components (`src/controls.rs`, used through `components/native.tsx`): `UIButton` with a glass configuration and `UISearchTextField` on interactive glass on iOS, `NSButton` with the glass bezel on macOS. Their glass answers the touch as the system's does, which nsh's `glassEffect` doesn't. The web, older systems and headless tests keep the app's look-alikes.
- **Window toolbar (macOS):** on the Mac the toolbar is a real `NSToolbar` (`src/toolbar.rs`, used through `lib/toolbar.ts`), which AppKit draws in Liquid Glass on macOS 26: the Library's sync, a prominent *+* in the app's red, and the system's search field (`NSSearchToolbarItem`, which ⌘F opens); the reader's Back and its actions, which AppKit groups into one capsule; the original page's Close, back, forward and reload. The titles stay the app's, beside them. Its items live in the title bar, outside nsh's root view, so clicks reach them as they reach any AppKit control (inside the root they don't: Findings 10). A page declares its items with `useToolbar`, and the newest page's are shown; clicks and search edits come back through `Toolbar.next()`, which the app keeps awaiting. Older macOS, the web and headless tests keep the app's own bars.
- **Share sheet:** nsh has none, so the app has a small Rust crate (`src/lib.rs`, `nsh.json` `"rust"`) exported to TSX as `lofu/native`: `UIActivityViewController` on iOS, `NSSharingServicePicker` on macOS (at the pointer), on the main thread. Elsewhere *Share…* isn't offered.
- **Saving:** *+* (the toolbar on desktops, the header on phones) saves a page by its address. The form starts from a link on the clipboard (iOS asks *Allow Paste?* first), and pasted share text ("【Title】 http://xhslink.com/a/… 复制本条信息…") becomes its link (`packages/shared/src/links.ts`). Short links (t.co, t.cn) are saved as the page they lead to, which the server finds. From a collection, a tag or Favorites, it saves there. The bookmark shows at once and fills in once the server has read the page.
- **Saving from other apps (iOS):** *lofu* in any app's share sheet saves the page there and then (`share/`, a Swift share extension): a link, or share text with a link inside (the extractor is a port of `extractUrl` in `packages/shared/src/links.ts`, which `packages/shared/test/links.test.ts` holds to the same cases). It saves into Unsorted, says *Saved* (or *Already saved*) and closes; a failure says why and offers *Try again*. It needs the app's server and token, which the app puts in a Keychain group the two share each time it starts or connects; before the app has been opened once, it says to open it.
- **Data:** a local mirror kept current with `/v1/sync` (`lib/store.ts`), so counts and filters are instant and the last snapshot shows at launch, offline too. It syncs when the app comes to the front and every five minutes while it's open. Favorite, move, delete, edits, highlights and saving apply at once. Offline they wait in an outbox kept on the device and are sent, in order, before the next sync (a bookmark saved offline gets an id made on the device, which the server keeps); a change the server refuses is undone. The sync line says how many changes are waiting. Search goes to the server (`/v1/search`, which knows the article text), shows its snippets with the matches marked and its total, and loads more as the list nears its end.
- **On the device:** files in the app's data directory (`src/files.rs`, `lib/files.ts`) hold the snapshot, past `Storage`'s 5 MB, and the articles kept for reading offline (`lib/articles.ts`): every article opened, and after each sync the ones *Reading offline* in Settings asks for (the 80 newest unread, everything unread, everything outside the Trash, or none), three at a time. Their pictures are kept beside them as files of the app's own (`picture-<id>-<hash of the URL>`), downloaded with `fetch`, rather than in nsh's image cache, which holds 256 MB for every image the app shows and which the system may clear (and `Image.prefetch` never settles on macOS in nsh 0.4.0). An article opens from its copy at once, its pictures from their files (an image's source may be a plain path: iOS doesn't decode a `file:` URL's escapes, and `allows_media` puts `file://` through the network policy), and is refreshed from the server. Settings says how many articles are kept and the room they take, downloads them now, or removes them; offline, cards of articles not kept say *Online only*. The web keeps no files. On iOS the API token lives in the Keychain (`Secrets`); macOS keeps it in `Storage`, since an unsigned build would ask for the keychain's password after every rebuild.
- **Editing:** *Edit title, tags and note…* in every bookmark's menu (and the pencil in the reader's toolbar): a dialog on desktops, a page on phones (`components/EditBookmark.tsx`). Tags are separated by commas and complete from the ones in use.
- **Collections and tags:** right-click (desktop) or long-press (phone) a collection for *Rename…*, *Move up*/*Move down* (in the server's own order) and *Delete collection…* (its bookmarks go to Unsorted), or a tag for *Rename or merge…* and *Delete tag…*. nsh's `AlertDialog` takes no text, so names are asked for on a card of the app's own (`components/Prompt.tsx`).
- **Mac menu bar:** New Bookmark (⌘N), Find (⌘F), Sync Now (⌘R), Settings (⌘,), the fixed views (⌘1–⌘5), and for the bookmark being read: favorite (⌘D), edit (⌘E), open original (⌘O), share, Move to Trash (⌘⌫).
- **Reader:** the article's Markdown in a read-only `RichEditor`, with the bookmark's note under the title (left out of the text highlights are anchored in). Saved highlights are drawn as inline decorations. Selecting text shows a **Highlight** bubble, and a press inside a highlight offers **Remove**. Highlights whose words the saved text no longer has still count; *N not found* lists their quotes, to keep or remove. A page lofu couldn't read offers *Try reading it again* and points to the browser extension.
- **Reading progress:** the editor reports no scrolling, so every few seconds the reader asks it which text is at the top of the view (`posAtPageCoords`, which answers once the pointer or a finger has been over the text) and keeps that share of the text as `readProgress`; reaching the end is 1. Opening the article again scrolls back there (to the end, then up to the place, so it lands at the top). Cards and rows show *42%* or *Read*.
- **Original page:** *Open original* shows the page in the app (`screens/Browser.tsx`, nsh's `WebView`: WKWebView on macOS and iOS) with back, forward, reload and *Open in browser*: a toolbar on the Mac, a bar along the bottom on the phone. The web build opens the system's browser instead, since most sites refuse to be framed.
- **Anchoring:** highlights are W3C text-quote selectors (the quote plus about 32 characters of context on each side), matched with whitespace ignored (`packages/shared/src/anchor.ts`). They survive the article being shown by a different renderer, and highlights imported without context (Raindrop) are placed too. `lib/docIndex.ts` maps between plain-text offsets and ProseMirror document positions.

## Run it

```sh
nsh run apps/reader                     # GTK window on Linux, AppKit on macOS
nsh run apps/reader/App.tsx --target web   # http://127.0.0.1:8790 (server needs LOFU_CORS_ORIGINS=http://127.0.0.1:8790)
nsh run apps/reader --target ios        # on a Mac with Xcode
```

Building now compiles `src/lib.rs` into the app's runner, so it needs Rust and the target's toolchain: `rustup target add aarch64-apple-ios aarch64-apple-ios-sim` for iOS, and for the web `wasm32-unknown-unknown` plus a clang that targets wasm (Homebrew's `llvm`; Apple's clang can't build nsh's `zstd-sys` for wasm). nsh 0.4.0's `nsh-platform-ios` from crates.io includes `../../nsh-platform-macos/src/speech.rs`, a path that only exists in nsh's repository; until that's fixed upstream, `ln -s nsh-platform-macos-0.4.0 ~/.cargo/registry/src/index.crates.io-*/nsh-platform-macos` lets the iOS build find it.

The first launch asks for the server address and a token, and checks them before saving. `nsh package App.tsx --target macos` makes a `.app`; `--target ios-simulator --run` installs one in the simulator. For a phone, `IPHONEOS_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET=17.0 nsh package App.tsx --target ios --sign "Apple Development: …" --profile <app.lofu.reader's .mobileprovision>`, then `xcrun devicectl device install app --device <udid> .nsh/out/lofu.ipa`: without the deployment target the runner links for iOS 10, which lacks the `___chkstk_darwin` QuickJS needs.

### With the share extension (iOS)

nsh can't build app extensions, so `share/package.ts` packages the app with `nsh package`, builds the extension with xcodegen and xcodebuild, puts it in `lofu.app/PlugIns/` and signs the app again, with the shared Keychain group added to its entitlements:

```sh
bun share/package.ts ios-simulator --install            # .nsh/out/lofu.app, installed in the booted simulator
bun share/package.ts ios --sign <identity or its SHA-1> --profile <app.lofu.reader's .mobileprovision> [--install --device <udid>]
```

It needs `xcodegen` (Homebrew). For a phone, Xcode has to be signed in to your team (Settings › Accounts): xcodebuild registers `app.lofu.reader.share` and makes its profile. On the simulator the entitlements go into the binaries (`__TEXT,__entitlements`, as Xcode does), passed to nsh's link through `RUSTFLAGS`, since an ad hoc signature with entitlements doesn't launch. A plain `nsh package` or `nsh run` makes an app without the extension; it works as before and has no Keychain group to share.

## Checks

```sh
cd apps/reader && nsh test                    # headless (QuickJS, as on devices)
nsh test --target web --browser <chromium>    # the same tests in a real browser
bun e2e/web.ts                                # the highlight loop with real mouse/keyboard input in Chrome
```

`e2e/web.ts` needs the web dev server, a lofu server with the test articles (listed at the top of the file), and `LOFU_URL`/`LOFU_TOKEN`. It writes screenshots to `e2e/screens/`.

The same flow on the real platforms, with the platform's own clicks and touches against a running server (some bookmarks in a few collections; the test opens the `MLDL` collection and "Mixture of Experts Explained"):

```sh
LOFU_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8787 LOFU_TOKEN=lofu_… bun e2e/apps/run.ts macos [light|dark]
LOFU_URL=… LOFU_TOKEN=… bun e2e/apps/run.ts ios [light|dark]       # the booted simulator
```

It signs in, searches, opens a collection and an article, and writes `e2e/screens/<platform>-<scheme>-*.png`. Look at them: layout regressions don't fail assertions.

## Findings (nsh 0.4.0)

What was verified on the **web target** with real input:

| Works | Notes |
|---|---|
| Long articles | 11,941 words visible about 150 ms after the tap; Wikipedia (8,125 words, 600+ runs) in about 180 ms, with a 6 MB JS heap. |
| CJK text | Chinese renders, selects and highlights. |
| Selection → highlight → reload → remove | `onSelectionChange`, the bubble menu, `textBetween`, and decorations that follow the text. |
| Saved highlights without context | Placed on load (Raindrop imports have no context). |

Problems found, with workarounds:

1. **Web: dragging the mouse doesn't select the right text in `RichEditor`** (read-only or editable). With the editor at the window's left edge a drag selected nothing; beside the sidebar it selects, but the wrong range: dragging across "The following recipe assumes you're very ambitious" selects "g recipe assumes you're very a". Shift+click and Shift+arrow keys select exactly, so highlighting works that way, and `e2e/web.ts` uses them. Upstream: [longbridge/nsh#1004](https://github.com/longbridge/nsh/issues/1004).
2. **Decoration `attrs.style` is ignored**: the text is split at the range but nothing is drawn. `attrs.class` works, so highlights use Tailwind classes.
3. **`RichEditor` loads Markdown, JSON or plain text, but not HTML.** That's fine, because the server keeps Markdown for every article. Raw HTML in the Markdown would show as literal text, so the server converts what Markdown can't express (SVG, `sup`, players, merged-cell tables) into plain Markdown, and the reader still strips any embed that slips through. Tables need `TableKit` (not in `StarterKit`). Footnotes have no nodes in `RichEditor` (only `TextView` has them): the parser drops `[^1]` and keeps a definition as a bare paragraph, so `lib/footnotes.ts` numbers the references, makes them superscript and lists the notes in order. The editor can't raise text, so the numbers are small and in the accent color instead, and they are kept out of the text highlights are anchored in.
4. **`TextView` (the obvious reading component) has no API for reading the selection.** The prototype therefore uses a read-only `RichEditor`, which has one.
5. **Passing a signal's `set` as a bare function** throws "not a live signal" in QuickJS (devices and headless tests) but works in a browser's JS engine. Wrap it: `(v) => sig.set(v)`.
6. **Web: `lifecycle()` is missing** from the web runtime (it's typed and documented): calling it throws at startup. The app checks for it and, on the web, syncs at launch and on request only.
7. **`List` recycles its cells**: a component's setup code runs once per cell, not per item, so anything it computes from props once (a status label, an image-failed flag) leaks to the next item scrolled in. Read props inside JSX or functions, and key per-item state by the item (`components/BookmarkCard.tsx` remembers *which* cover URL failed).
8. **Liquid Glass (`glassEffect`) ignores the view's `backgroundColor`.** The app tints its glass with a translucent child layer instead (`GlassTint` in `components/ui.tsx`), drawn only where the system glass is (iOS and macOS 26+); elsewhere `glass()` falls back to a blurred, mostly opaque paper. `nsh test` screenshots show the glass's base color, not what is behind it.
9. **A page with `headerTransparent` gets no scroll edge effect.** `List` insets itself under the bar, `RichEditor` doesn't (the reader pads its content), and neither gets iOS 26's blur under the bar, so `TopEdge` draws one (a backdrop blur with a gradient mask). Without `headerTransparent` and without a header color, the system bar is opaque white.
10. **macOS: a click on a native component's own view is lost.** nsh's picking finds the hosted view, but the root's `hitTest:` never hands it the event, and the root then drops the press as the hosted view's (VoiceOver's press still reaches it). So on the Mac a clear view of the app's lies over each system button and takes the click, and the toolbar's buttons and search field are a real `NSToolbar`'s, in the title bar, where clicks arrive (`NSSearchField` in the content couldn't be clicked into).

On **macOS and iOS** (Xcode 27 beta, an iPhone Air simulator on iOS 27), with the platforms' own clicks and touches (`e2e/apps`): signing in, the sidebar or the Library page, collections, server search with marked snippets, opening an article, and light and dark mode all work, as do native back (swipe, the header's back button, Escape and Cmd+[ on the Mac) and the system header on iOS (`headerRightSymbol`, large titles).

**Not verified yet:**
- **Selecting text with a long press (iOS) or a drag (macOS), and so highlighting there.** `nsh test`'s synthetic long press and drag over the read-only `RichEditor` select nothing, and on this machine the simulator can't be driven otherwise (`agent-device` needs an Xcode whose simulator service loads on macOS 27.3). Try it by hand: open an article, long-press a word, and look for the **Highlight** bubble.
- **Linux GTK: deferred.** A Linux desktop app isn't a goal for now. Verifying it would need a headless X server (`xorg-server-xvfb` and `xdotool`).
