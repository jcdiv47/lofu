# lofu app (Android, macOS, iOS, web)

The lofu app on [Native Shell](https://nsh.dev) (nsh 0.5.1): one TSX codebase that runs as a native app on Android, an AppKit app on macOS, a UIKit app on iOS, and in the browser. Android packaging, installation, and physical-device tests are documented in [android/README.md](android/README.md). It started as a prototype answering **can an nsh app show a saved article, let you highlight passages, and put the highlights back in the right place?** (yes, see Findings).

- **Layout:** on a Mac (or any other window 640+ points wide, not a phone) a sidebar of Liquid Glass sits beside a card grid whose toolbar floats over it, in a window with the app's own title bar. The Mac's window keeps at least 800 × 480 points, as Finder's keeps a minimum, so it always has the sidebar: the phone's page isn't made for a window. On a phone it's a native navigation stack that opens on *Library* (`screens/Home.tsx`), which has round glass buttons for settings and a menu (new collection, fold or unfold every section, sort collections and tags, all kept on the device), inset groups for collections, filters (favorites, highlights and the kinds of page: articles, videos, documents…) and tags that fold away, the Trash last, and search and *+* on glass at the bottom. Typing in its search narrows the page to the collections, filters and tags whose names match, under a row that searches the bookmarks' text.
- **Library:** *All bookmarks*, *Unsorted*, *Favorites*, *Highlights*, collections and tags, with counts, and the *Trash* at the foot of the sidebar or the Library page. Cards show the page's cover, or a catalog card with the excerpt in serif when it has none. Pages the server couldn't read are marked *Unreadable*. Right-click (desktop) or long-press (phone) for the three ways to open it (*Open in Reader*, the saved text; *Open Original*, the page in the app; *Open in Browser*, the system's), Copy Link, Share…, Favorite, Mark as Read, Edit, Move to (a collection or Unsorted, the one it's in checked), Try Reading It Again, and Delete. A tap opens it as *Settings › Open bookmarks in* says (the reader unless changed). The reader's ••• has the two ways to open the original and what its toolbar has no button for; deleting from it goes back to the list. On iOS that's the system's own menu in Liquid Glass (`UIMenu`, `PopupMenu` in `src/lib.rs`), growing out of where the finger is, a swipe's *Move* and *More*, the reader's •••, or the Library page's ••• (with the sorts as submenus, the current one checked): grouped, with symbols, the ways to open it as a row of tiles at the top (`preferredElementSize` medium), and the collections as a submenu of *Move to*. It is the menu of a clear `UIButton` laid there while it shows (a button's menu is glass and leaves the screen undimmed). UIKit has no public call that opens a button's menu, so the app asks the button's `UIContextMenuInteraction` with `_presentMenuAtLocation:`; should a future iOS drop that call, the app falls back to nsh's action sheet.
- **Swipes (phones):** a row swiped right shows *Move* (*Put back* in the Trash); swiped left, *Share…*, *Delete* and *More* (`components/SwipeRow.tsx`). The drag is a native pan bound to the horizontal axis only, so the list still scrolls; one row is open at a time and a tap shuts it.
- **Trash:** *Delete* moves a bookmark to the Trash, which is what the server calls `archived` (bookmarks archived before are there too). Deleting from the Trash asks, then deletes it for good: `DELETE /v1/bookmarks/{id}`, a tombstone that other devices drop on sync. Search leaves the Trash out, except in the Trash.
- **Sort and view (wide windows):** as Raindrop's, beside the library's title: *Sort by* (*Manual (Drag'n'drop)* in a collection and Unsorted, which start in it; By date ↑/↓, By name A-Z/Z-A, Sites A-Z/Z-A) and *View* (List, Cards, Headlines, Moodboard, and *Apply to all*), each source keeping its own on the device (`lib/prefs.ts`, `components/ListControls.tsx`). Under the views, *Show in …* picks what each view shows (Cover, Title, Note, Description, Highlights (their count), Tags, Bookmark info), then the covers' size (Cards, Moodboard: a slider on the web, a submenu on the Mac) or *Cover position* (List). A search keeps the server's order, best match first, and hides *Sort by*. On the Mac they're symbols in the window toolbar, as Finder's, opening the system's menu (the title row lies under that toolbar, which takes its clicks); the web draws its own menu with a radio mark and an icon per choice. Moodboard is a masonry: the bookmarks dealt to columns in turn, each column a `List` laid out in one scroll view (`viewport="parent"`), the covers at their own shape.
- **View, sort and filter (phones):** the list's ••• in the bar (the system's menu on iOS, `phoneListMenu` in `components/ListControls.tsx`; a row of the app's own menus over the list on the web and Android) has the same views, at the top with the current one checked, then *Sort by*, *Filter*, *Show in …*, the cover's size or position and *Apply to All*, each submenu naming its current choice. A phone starts in the list it always had (its own *Show in List*: the excerpt, no note or tags), and the cards and moodboard take two columns in portrait at the default size, more on their side or with smaller covers, one when the text is large (Dynamic Type). *Filter* (`lib/filters.ts`) narrows the list open to Unread (not yet read to the end), Favorites, With highlights, With a note or Without tags, and to one kind of page, each with how many it leaves; the filter shows as chips over the list (*Unread ✕*, and "120 of 899"), lasts while the page is open, and a search keeps it, sending the server the conditions it knows (`type:`, `favorite:true`…; Unread is applied on the device). A search shows *Searching…* while the server is asked; offline, or after 15 s without an answer, it matches titles, addresses, descriptions, notes and tags on the device instead, and says so. Each view is a scroll view of its own; nsh hands UIKit's inset and large-title tracking only to the scroll view a page mounts with, so a view switched to later is handed over by `lofu.scrollEdge` (`src/edges.rs`), with the first one's inset behaviour and at the top under the large title.
- **Manual order:** in *Manual*, a bookmark is dragged to its place in any view (`components/ManualDrag.tsx`): it follows the pointer, a line in the accent colour marks where it lands, and the cells on screen are measured as the drag starts, so it drops among those in view (lists recycle their cells). The place is the server's `sort` (migration 4: minus the save time, so the order starts newest first and new bookmarks go on top); a drop sets it to the midpoint of the new neighbours, one PATCH (`lib/manual.ts`). Bookmarks imported in the same millisecond share a place: a drop among them spaces out only the smallest stretch around it with room. Ties sort by id, the same on every device.
- **System controls:** on iOS 26 and macOS 26 the round glass buttons (*+*, Back, Close), the labelled ones (*Open original*, *Cancel*) and, on iOS, the search field are the system's own, registered from Rust as native components (`src/controls.rs`, used through `components/native.tsx`): `UIButton` with a glass configuration and `UISearchTextField` on interactive glass on iOS, `NSButton` with the glass bezel on macOS. Their glass answers the touch as the system's does, which nsh's `glassEffect` doesn't. On iOS the reader's *Highlight* bubble and highlight count are system glass buttons too, the original page's bar along the bottom is a row of system buttons on one capsule of interactive glass, as Safari's (`lofu.glassBar`, `src/glassbar.rs`), and names (a new collection, *Rename…*) are asked for with the system's alert, which iOS 26 draws in glass (`TextPrompt`, `src/prompt.rs`). The scroll edge is the system's on iOS 26: the page blurs and fades out under the navigation bar and under the controls floating at its foot, as UIKit draws it (`lofu.scrollEdge`, `src/edges.rs`; Findings 9). On the Mac, *Settings* (⌘,) is a dialog on the system's glass laid out as System Settings: titled groups of rows, each choice a pop-up button that opens the system's menu, and the system's glass buttons (`controlSize` makes them smaller than a toolbar's). The web, older systems and headless tests keep the app's look-alikes; on phones and the web the choices are rows of their own, the current one checked.
- **Sidebar (macOS):** on macOS 26 and later the sidebar is the system's own: an `NSSplitViewController` sidebar item (`src/sidebar.rs`, `Sidebar` in `lofu/native`), which AppKit draws in Liquid Glass, flush with the window's edges as Finder's, with its own edge and inactive look. nsh owns the window's content view, so the split view lies under it in the window's frame view and only draws: nsh's root is transparent over it and draws the rows, which take every click. nsh paints the root's holder black or white for the scheme, which would hide the glass; the module clears it when it goes in and after every appearance change. The web and older systems keep the app's floating glass panel.
- **Window toolbar (macOS):** on the Mac the toolbar is a real `NSToolbar` (`src/toolbar.rs`, used through `lib/toolbar.ts`), which AppKit draws in Liquid Glass on macOS 26: the Library's sync, a prominent *+* in the app's red, and the system's search field (`NSSearchToolbarItem`, which ⌘F opens); the reader's Back and its actions, which AppKit groups into one capsule; the original page's Close, back, forward and reload. The titles stay the app's, beside them. Its items live in the title bar, outside nsh's root view, so clicks reach them as they reach any AppKit control (inside the root they don't: Findings 10). As the window narrows, AppKit narrows the search field and then folds it into a button rather than lay the items over the library's title (a `space` keeps the title's room), and the title truncates where the items start, which the toolbar measures (a `mark`). The reader's and the original page's titles start where Back, or the page's controls, end, measured the same way: AppKit gives the space before them a slot of at least 36 points, so they sit further in than the space asks. A page declares its items with `useToolbar`, and the newest page's are shown; clicks and search edits come back through `Toolbar.next()`, which the app keeps awaiting. Older macOS, the web and headless tests keep the app's own bars.
- **Share sheet:** nsh has none, so the app has a small Rust crate (`src/lib.rs`, `nsh.json` `"rust"`) exported to TSX as `lofu/native`: `UIActivityViewController` on iOS, `NSSharingServicePicker` on macOS (at the pointer), on the main thread. Elsewhere *Share…* isn't offered.
- **Saving:** *+* (the toolbar on desktops, the header on phones) saves a page by its address. The form starts from a link on the clipboard (iOS asks *Allow Paste?* first), and pasted share text ("【Title】 http://xhslink.com/a/… 复制本条信息…") becomes its link (`packages/shared/src/links.ts`). Short links (t.co, t.cn) are saved as the page they lead to, which the server finds. From a collection, a tag or Favorites, it saves there. The bookmark shows at once and fills in once the server has read the page.
- **Saving from other apps (iOS):** *lofu* in any app's share sheet saves the page there and then (`share/`, a Swift share extension): a link, or share text with a link inside (the extractor is a port of `extractUrl` in `packages/shared/src/links.ts`, which `packages/shared/test/links.test.ts` holds to the same cases). It saves into Unsorted, says *Saved* (or *Already saved*) and closes; a failure says why and offers *Try again*. It needs the app's server and token, which the app puts in a Keychain group the two share each time it starts or connects; before the app has been opened once, it says to open it.
- **Data:** a local mirror kept current with `/v1/sync` (`lib/store.ts`), so counts and filters are instant and the last snapshot shows at launch, offline too. It syncs when the app comes to the front and every five minutes while it's open. Favorite, move, delete, edits, highlights and saving apply at once. Offline they wait in an outbox kept on the device and are sent, in order, before the next sync (a bookmark saved offline gets an id made on the device, which the server keeps); a change the server refuses is undone. The sync line says how many changes are waiting. Search goes to the server (`/v1/search`, which knows the article text), shows its snippets with the matches marked and its total, and loads more as the list nears its end.
- **On the device:** files in the app's data directory (`src/files.rs`, `lib/files.ts`) hold the snapshot, past `Storage`'s 5 MB, and the articles kept for reading offline (`lib/articles.ts`): every article opened, and after each sync the ones *Reading offline* in Settings asks for (the 80 newest unread, everything unread, everything outside the Trash, or none), three at a time. Their pictures are kept beside them as files of the app's own (`picture-<id>-<hash of the URL>`), downloaded with `fetch`, rather than in nsh's image cache, which holds 256 MB for every image the app shows and which the system may clear (and `Image.prefetch` never settles on macOS in nsh 0.4.0; 0.5.0 left it as it was). An article opens from its copy at once, its pictures from their files (an image's source may be a plain path: iOS doesn't decode a `file:` URL's escapes, and `allows_media` puts `file://` through the network policy), and is refreshed from the server. Settings says how many articles are kept and the room they take, downloads them now, or removes them; offline, cards of articles not kept say *Online only*. The web keeps no files. On iOS the API token lives in the Keychain (`Secrets`); macOS keeps it in `Storage`, since an unsigned build would ask for the keychain's password after every rebuild.
- **Editing:** *Edit title, tags and note…* in every bookmark's menu (and the pencil in the reader's toolbar): a dialog on desktops, a page on phones (`components/EditBookmark.tsx`). Tags are separated by commas and complete from the ones in use.
- **Collections and tags:** a collection is made with the *+* on the sidebar's *Collections* heading (File › New Collection…, ⇧⌘N, on the Mac; the Library page's ••• on phones) and opens once made. A tag is made by giving it to a bookmark (*Edit…*): the server keeps tags on bookmarks only, so there are no empty ones. Right-click (desktop) or long-press (phone) a collection for *Rename…*, *Move up*/*Move down* (in the server's own order) and *Delete collection…* (its bookmarks go to Unsorted), or a tag for *Rename or merge…* and *Delete tag…*. nsh's `AlertDialog` takes no text, so names are asked for with the system's alert on iOS (`src/prompt.rs`) and on a card of the app's own elsewhere (`components/Prompt.tsx`).
- **Mac menu bar:** New Bookmark (⌘N), New Collection (⇧⌘N), Find (⌘F), Sync Now (⌘R), Settings (⌘,), the fixed views (⌘1–⌘5), and for the bookmark being read: favorite (⌘D), edit (⌘E), open original (⌘O), open in browser (⇧⌘O), share, Move to Trash (⌘⌫).
- **Reader:** the article's Markdown in a read-only `RichEditor`, with the bookmark's note under the title (left out of the text highlights are anchored in). Saved highlights are drawn as inline decorations. Selecting text shows a **Highlight** bubble, and a press inside a highlight offers **Remove**. Highlights whose words the saved text no longer has still count; *N not found* lists their quotes, to keep or remove. A page lofu couldn't read offers *Try reading it again* and points to the browser extension. Links are underlined in the accent colour and open in the system's browser when pressed (nsh's `link()` plugin: `StarterKit`'s link is only the mark). On iOS the editor focuses a hidden text view of nsh's on every press, read-only or not, which raised the keyboard; the app gives that view an empty `inputView` (`Keyboard` in `src/lib.rs`), so it keeps the focus and the keyboard stays down.
- **Long articles:** shown a page at a time (`present` in `screens/Reader.tsx`). nsh's `RichEditor` makes a native view of every node of its document at once and lays them all out again on every change, so a GitHub README of 6,600 nodes and 471 pictures held the Mac's window still for over 8 s (the development host) before its first line showed. Now the first ~300 nodes are shown and the next 150 added at the end (`insertContentAt`) as the end of the text comes within two screens of the view, asked a few times a second with `pageCoordsAtPos`; until the pointer or a finger has been over the text that can't be told, and a page is added every 1.5 s. Adding at the end moves nothing before it, so the text index and highlights are the whole document's from the start (`Article.test.tsx`); a highlight is drawn once its page is in. Reopening at the reader's place first adds the pages up to a little past it. That README's first line now shows about 0.4 s after the click.
- **Reading progress:** the editor reports no scrolling, so every few seconds the reader asks it which text is at the top of the view (`posAtPageCoords`, which answers once the pointer or a finger has been over the text) and keeps that share of the text as `readProgress`; reaching the end is 1. Opening the article again opens it there: the text stays hidden while the editor lays it out and moves to the place (just under the bars), then fades in over 150 ms (at once with reduced motion), so the way there is never seen; it shows after a second whatever happens. Cards and rows show *42%* or *Read*.
- **Original page:** *Open Original* (the globe) shows the page in the app (`screens/Browser.tsx`, nsh's `WebView`: WKWebView on macOS and iOS) with back, forward, reload and *Open in browser* (the compass): a toolbar on the Mac, a bar along the bottom on the phone. The web build opens the system's browser instead, since most sites refuse to be framed.
- **Anchoring:** highlights are W3C text-quote selectors (the quote plus about 32 characters of context on each side), matched with whitespace ignored (`packages/shared/src/anchor.ts`). They survive the article being shown by a different renderer, and highlights imported without context (Raindrop) are placed too. `lib/docIndex.ts` maps between plain-text offsets and ProseMirror document positions.

## Run it

```sh
nsh run apps/reader                     # GTK window on Linux, AppKit on macOS
nsh run apps/reader/App.tsx --target web   # http://127.0.0.1:8790 (server needs LOFU_CORS_ORIGINS=http://127.0.0.1:8790)
nsh run apps/reader --target ios        # on a Mac with Xcode
```

Building now compiles `src/lib.rs` into the app's runner, so it needs Rust and the target's toolchain: `rustup target add aarch64-apple-ios aarch64-apple-ios-sim` for iOS, and for the web `wasm32-unknown-unknown` plus a clang that targets wasm (Homebrew's `llvm`; Apple's clang can't build nsh's `zstd-sys` for wasm).

The first launch asks for the server address and a token, and checks them before saving. `nsh package App.tsx --target macos` makes a `.app`; `--target ios-simulator --run` installs one in the simulator. For a phone, `IPHONEOS_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET=17.0 nsh package App.tsx --target ios --sign "Apple Development: …" --profile <app.lofu.reader's .mobileprovision>`, then `xcrun devicectl device install app --device <udid> .nsh/out/lofu.ipa`: without the deployment target the runner links for iOS 10, which lacks the `___chkstk_darwin` QuickJS needs.

The app's icon is `design/logo/logo.png`, which `nsh.json` names: every `nsh package` target takes it from there, the web build's favicon and touch icon too. The browser extension's icons come from the same folder (`bun run --cwd apps/extension icons`).

### With the share extension (iOS)

nsh can't build app extensions, so `share/package.ts` packages the app with `nsh package`, builds the extension with xcodegen and xcodebuild, puts it in `lofu.app/PlugIns/` and signs the app again, with the shared Keychain group added to its entitlements:

```sh
bun share/package.ts ios-simulator --install            # .nsh/out/lofu.app, installed in the booted simulator
bun share/package.ts ios --sign <identity or its SHA-1> --profile <app.lofu.reader's .mobileprovision> [--install --device <udid>]
```

It needs `xcodegen` (Homebrew). For a phone, Xcode has to be signed in to your team (Settings › Accounts): xcodebuild registers `app.lofu.reader.share` and makes its profile. On the simulator the entitlements go into the binaries (`__TEXT,__entitlements`, as Xcode does), passed to nsh's link through `RUSTFLAGS`, since an ad hoc signature with entitlements doesn't launch. A plain `nsh package` or `nsh run` makes an app without the extension; it works as before and has no Keychain group to share.

## Checks

```sh
cd apps/reader && nsh test                    # headless (QuickJS, as on devices)
nsh test --target web --browser <chromium>    # the same tests in a real browser
bun e2e/web.ts                                # the highlight loop with real mouse/keyboard input in Chrome
```

`e2e/web.ts` needs the web dev server, a lofu server with the test articles (listed at the top of the file), and `LOFU_URL`/`LOFU_TOKEN`. It writes screenshots to `e2e/screens/`.

The same flow on the real platforms, with the platform's own clicks and touches against a running server (some bookmarks in a few collections; the test opens the `MLDL` collection and "Mixture of Experts Explained"):

```sh
LOFU_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8787 LOFU_TOKEN=lofu_… bun e2e/apps/run.ts macos [light|dark]
LOFU_URL=… LOFU_TOKEN=… bun e2e/apps/run.ts ios [light|dark]       # the booted simulator
```

It signs in, searches, opens a collection and an article, and writes `e2e/screens/<platform>-<scheme>-*.png`. Look at them: layout regressions don't fail assertions.

## Findings (nsh 0.4.0, checked again on 0.5.0)

What was verified on the **web target** with real input:

| Works | Notes |
|---|---|
| Long articles | 11,941 words visible about 150 ms after the tap; Wikipedia (8,125 words, 600+ runs) in about 180 ms, with a 6 MB JS heap. |
| CJK text | Chinese renders, selects and highlights. |
| Selection → highlight → reload → remove | `onSelectionChange`, the bubble menu, `textBetween`, and decorations that follow the text. |
| Saved highlights without context | Placed on load (Raindrop imports have no context). |

Problems found, with workarounds:

1. **Decoration `attrs.style` is ignored**: the text is split at the range but nothing is drawn. `attrs.class` works, so highlights use Tailwind classes.
2. **`RichEditor` loads Markdown, JSON, plain text and (since 0.5) HTML, and its Markdown takes no raw HTML.** The reader loads Markdown, because the server keeps Markdown for every article. Raw HTML in the Markdown would show as literal text, so the server converts what Markdown can't express (SVG, `sup`, players, merged-cell tables) into plain Markdown, and the reader still strips any embed that slips through. Tables need `TableKit` (not in `StarterKit`). Footnotes have no nodes in `RichEditor` (only `TextView` has them): the parser drops `[^1]` and keeps a definition as a bare paragraph, so `lib/footnotes.ts` numbers the references, makes them superscript and lists the notes in order. The editor can't raise text, so the numbers are small and in the accent color instead, and they are kept out of the text highlights are anchored in. Formulas have no node either (only `TextView` typesets TeX), and the parser would show `$…$` as it is and read `*` and `_` in it as emphasis. So `lib/math.ts` takes each formula out before parsing and sets it afterwards as text: variables in italics, symbols as characters, scripts as Unicode's raised and lowered characters when there's at most one letter and the character looks right in the fallback fonts (xₜ, hₜ₋₁, ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ; the serif has almost none of them), and otherwise as smaller text (marks `mathSub`/`mathSup`), a base's subscript and superscript always alike, fractions as a/b. Every formula is the server's picture of it (`formulas` in the article: SVG drawn by MathJax, in the ink of the appearance, kept for offline reading in both inks): on a line of its own centred, in text lowered by its depth so it sits on the baseline (a picture's bottom rests on it; the depth is the image's `title`, which a class per pixel translates down; every formula has one, depth 0 too, as on the Mac an SVG image node no class applies to draws nothing). A change of appearance draws them again in the other ink in the editor already shown (the view takes its session once). Without one (an older server, TeX MathJax can't read) it is set as text, cases and aligned equations a row a line. A picture can't be highlighted inside. The Markdown keeps the TeX for the CLI and agents.
3. **`TextView` (the obvious reading component) can't hold highlights.** In 0.4 it had no API for reading the selection; 0.5 reads it, but selects each block on its own (no passage across paragraphs) and has no decorations or positions. The reader therefore uses a read-only `RichEditor`, which has them.
4. **`List` recycles its cells**: a component's setup code runs once per cell, not per item, so anything it computes from props once (a status label, an image-failed flag) leaks to the next item scrolled in. Read props inside JSX or functions, and key per-item state by the item (`components/BookmarkCard.tsx` remembers *which* cover URL failed).
5. **Liquid Glass (`glassEffect`) ignores the view's `backgroundColor`.** The app tints its glass with a translucent child layer instead (`GlassTint` in `components/ui.tsx`), drawn only where the system glass is (iOS and macOS 26+); elsewhere `glass()` falls back to a blurred, mostly opaque paper. 0.5 adds `glassTintColor`, which colours the system glass itself rather than laying a tint over it; not tried yet. `nsh test` screenshots show the glass's base color, not what is behind it.
6. **A page with `headerTransparent` gets no scroll edge effect.** `List` insets itself under the bar, `RichEditor` doesn't (the reader pads its content), and neither gets iOS 26's blur under the bar, so `TopEdge` draws one (a backdrop blur with a gradient mask). Without `headerTransparent` and without a header color, the system bar is opaque white. On iOS 26 the app hands UIKit the page's scroll view itself (`src/edges.rs`): the screen's controller gets it (`setContentScrollView:forEdge:`) and the navigation bar, and the row of controls at a page's foot, get a `UIScrollEdgeElementContainerInteraction` for it, so UIKit draws its own edge effect, shaped around the bar's items (on the Library page, whose bar has only its two round buttons, the effect is shallow, as UIKit draws it for any bar like that). `TopEdge` and `BottomEdge` remain for the web and older systems.
7. **macOS: a click on a native component's own view is lost** (still in 0.5). nsh's picking finds the hosted view, but the root's `hitTest:` never hands it the event, and the root then drops the press as the hosted view's (VoiceOver's press still reaches it). So on the Mac a clear view of the app's lies over each system button and takes the click, and the toolbar's buttons and search field are a real `NSToolbar`'s, in the title bar, where clicks arrive (`NSSearchField` in the content couldn't be clicked into).

Fixed in nsh 0.5.1: article pictures in a packaged macOS app (a release build) were laid out but never drawn, from files and the network alike; 0.5.1 no longer culls views inside text (attachments). Still worked around: a paragraph of fixed line height grows a line holding a picture as much again below its baseline on macOS, so paragraphs of pictures only get no line height (`markPictureParagraphs` in `screens/Reader.tsx`).

Fixed in nsh 0.5.0, and their workarounds removed: drag-to-select on the web ([longbridge/nsh#1004](https://github.com/longbridge/nsh/issues/1004); `e2e/web.ts` selects with real drags now), pull-to-refresh under an iOS 26 large title ([longbridge/nsh#1418](https://github.com/longbridge/nsh/issues/1418); checked on the iOS 27.3 simulator), `lifecycle()` on the web, and the window's hidden title bar and minimum size (`nsh.json` `window` now). A signal's `set` is bound in 0.5 too (longbridge/nsh#1119), so passing it as a bare function no longer throws "not a live signal" in QuickJS; the app's `(v) => sig.set(v)` wrappers are harmless.

On **macOS and iOS** (Xcode 27 beta, an iPhone Air simulator on iOS 27), with the platforms' own clicks and touches (`e2e/apps`): signing in, the sidebar or the Library page, collections, server search with marked snippets, opening an article, and light and dark mode all work, as do native back (swipe, the header's back button, Escape and Cmd+[ on the Mac) and the system header on iOS (`headerRightSymbol`, large titles).

**Not verified yet:**
- **Selecting text with a long press (iOS) or a drag (macOS), and so highlighting there.** On nsh 0.5.0 in the iOS 27.3 simulator, a long press on a word brings up UIKit's edit menu (*Select All*) but selects nothing, so no **Highlight** bubble shows. `nsh test`'s synthetic long press and drag over the read-only `RichEditor` select nothing too. Try it by hand: open an article, long-press a word, and look for the **Highlight** bubble.
- **Linux GTK: deferred.** A Linux desktop app isn't a goal for now. Verifying it would need a headless X server (`xorg-server-xvfb` and `xdotool`).
